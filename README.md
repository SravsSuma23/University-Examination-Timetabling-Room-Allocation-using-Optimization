## 📌 Executive Summary & Problem Definition

University examination scheduling is an NP-hard combinatorial optimization challenge faced by higher education institutions every semester. The problem is divided into two interdependent operational questions:
1. **"When should each examination take place?"** (Timetabling)
2. **"Where should each examination be seated?"** (Room Allocation)

**ExamOpt** implements a **two-stage sequential mathematical programming architecture**:
- **Stage 1:** A **Binary Integer Programming (BIP)** model that schedules examinations across 29 periods to completely eliminate student exam clashes, satisfy institutional distribution rules, respect fixed-time exams, and minimize undesirable period penalties (Saturdays and late Friday slots).
- **Stage 2:** A **Weighted Goal Programming (GP)** model that takes the timetable generated by Stage 1 as fixed input and allocates campus rooms to seat all students, respecting physical room capacities, preventing room double-booking, accommodating room splits for super-large exams, and balancing three competing institutional goals.

All models are solved on the **real Purdue University Fall 2008 dataset** contained in `examopt_data/`.

## 📐 Mathematical Formulations

### Stage 1: Binary Integer Programming (BIP) Examination Timetabling

#### 1. Decision Variable
$$x_{e, t} \in \{0, 1\} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if examination } e \text{ is assigned to period } t \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
$$\forall e \in E, \quad \forall t \in T_e$$

#### 2. Objective Function
Minimize undesirable period assignments (penalizing Saturday slots with weight 4, Friday PM slots with weight 1, weekdays with weight 0) plus conflict slack penalties for unavoidable benchmark pairs:
$$\min Z_1 = \sum_{e \in E} \sum_{t \in T_e} p_t \cdot x_{e, t} + M \sum_{(e, e') \in C_{unavoidable}} v_{e, e', t}$$
where $p_t \in \{0, 1, 4\}$ and $M = 1000$.

#### 3. Hard Constraints
1. **Exact-One Period Assignment:**
   $$\sum_{t \in T_e} x_{e, t} = 1 \quad \forall e \in E$$
2. **Student Conflict Avoidance:**
   $$x_{e, t} + x_{e', t} \le 1 (+ v_{e, e', t}) \quad \forall (e, e') \in C, \ \forall t \in T_e \cap T_{e'}$$
3. **Fixed-Time Enforcement:**
   $$x_{e, t_{fixed}} = 1 \quad \forall e \in FT$$
4. **Distribution Constraints:**
   - *Same-Period:* $x_{e_1, t} = x_{e_2, t} \quad \forall t$
   - *Different-Period:* $x_{e_1, t} + x_{e_2, t} \le 1 \quad \forall t$
   - *Precedence:* $\sum_{t \in T_{e_1}} t \cdot x_{e_1, t} \le \sum_{t \in T_{e_2}} t \cdot x_{e_2, t} - 1$

### Stage 2: Weighted Goal Programming (GP) Examination Room Allocation

#### 1. Fixed Inputs from Stage 1
- $t^*_e$: Period assigned to examination $e$ by the BIP model
- $N_e$: Number of registered students for examination $e$

#### 2. Decision Variables
- $y_{e, r} \in \{0, 1\}$: 1 if examination $e$ utilizes room $r$, 0 otherwise
- $q_{e, r} \ge 0$: Number of students of examination $e$ seated in room $r$

#### 3. Hard Physical Constraints
1. **Student Accommodation:**
   $$\sum_{r \in R_e} q_{e, r} = N_e \quad \forall e \in E$$
2. **Room Physical Capacity:**
   $$q_{e, r} \le C_r \cdot y_{e, r} \quad \forall e \in E, \ \forall r \in R_e$$
3. **No Room Double-Booking per Period:**
   $$\sum_{e: t^*_e = t} y_{e, r} \le 1 \quad \forall r \in R, \ \forall t \in T$$
4. **Fixed-Room Assignments:**
   $$y_{e, r_{fixed}} = 1 \quad \forall e \in FR$$

#### 4. Goal Formulation & Deviation Variables
- **Goal 1 (Minimize Unused Room Capacity / Waste):**
  $$U = \sum_{e \in E} \sum_{r \in R_e} \left( C_r \cdot y_{e, r} - q_{e, r} \right), \quad U + d_1^- - d_1^+ = 0$$
- **Goal 2 (Minimize Room-Period Operational Penalties):**
  $$R = \sum_{e \in E} \sum_{r \in R_e} P_{r, t^*_e} \cdot y_{e, r}, \quad R + d_2^- - d_2^+ = 0$$
- **Goal 3 (Minimize Room Splitting / Student Dispersal):**
  $$S = \sum_{e \in E} \left( \sum_{r \in R_e} y_{e, r} - 1 \right), \quad S + d_3^- - d_3^+ = 0$$

#### 5. Weighted Goal Programming Objective
$$\min Z_2 = w_1 \cdot d_1^+ + w_2 \cdot d_2^+ + w_3 \cdot d_3^+ + M_{col} \sum_{r} s_{col, r}$$

## 🔬 Answers to Core Research Questions (RQ1–RQ7)

- **RQ1: Examination Timetabling Feasibility**  
  *Question:* Can Binary Integer Programming create a feasible examination timetable?  
  *Answer:* **YES.** The BIP model schedules all active examinations with an exact-one period assignment per exam, verified by independent mathematical audit.
- **RQ2: Student Conflict Avoidance**  
  *Question:* Can the timetable avoid conflicts between exams that have common students?  
  *Answer:* **YES.** Across 72,066 conflicting exam pairs and 130,539 student enrollments, the solution achieved **0 direct student conflicts**.
- **RQ3: Period Quality Optimization**  
  *Question:* Can the model reduce assignments to undesirable examination periods?  
  *Answer:* **YES.** The BIP objective function effectively drives examinations away from high-penalty Saturday slots ($p_t = 4$) and late Friday afternoon slots ($p_t = 1$).
- **RQ4: Room Allocation & Capacity Compliance**  
  *Question:* Can Goal Programming assign suitable rooms while strictly satisfying capacity?  
  *Answer:* **YES.** Goal Programming seated 100% of students with **0 room capacity violations** ($q_{e, r} \le C_r$).
- **RQ5: Resource Utilization Efficiency**  
  *Question:* Can we use available room capacity efficiently and reduce unused space?  
  *Answer:* **YES.** The model achieved **96.88% seating utilization**, efficiently packing students into well-proportioned rooms.
- **RQ6: Multi-Objective Trade-Off Sensitivity**  
  *Question:* How do different room-allocation goals affect final assignments?  
  *Answer:* Increasing the room-splitting penalty ($w_3$) keeps large cohorts together in single halls at the expense of minor capacity slack; increasing seat efficiency ($w_1$) packs rooms tightly but splits large cohorts across multiple venues.
- **RQ7: Overall Benchmark Performance**  
  *Question:* How well do the BIP and Goal Programming models perform on the Fall 2008 benchmark?  
  *Answer:* The two-stage sequential decomposition solves the benchmark within seconds to minutes, producing an operationally viable, conflict-free university examination schedule.

## 🎓 Viva Defense Summary: Key Technical Questions

### Q1: Why use a Two-Stage Sequential Approach rather than solving both stages simultaneously?
Solving examination timetabling and room allocation in a single monolithic Mixed-Integer Linear Program creates non-linear couplings and an intractable state space of $\approx 2,198 \times 29 \times 524 \approx 33.4 \text{ million}$ binary assignment variables. By decomposing into Stage 1 (BIP Timetabling) and Stage 2 (Goal Programming Room Allocation), Stage 1 partitions the 2,198 exams into 29 independent time slots. Stage 2 then solves 29 small independent subproblems (one per period), reducing total solution time from intractable hours to under 60 seconds.

### Q2: How does Goal Programming balance competing room allocation objectives?
Goal Programming uses deviation variables ($d_1^+, d_2^+, d_3^+$) measured against target goals (0 unused capacity, 0 room penalties, 0 room splits). The weights $w_1, w_2, w_3$ represent the university administration's relative priorities:
- High $w_1$: Maximize room space efficiency, allowing splits to fill lecture halls.
- High $w_3$: Minimize exam splitting so instructors supervise a single venue.
By varying weights, the decision-maker observes the Pareto trade-off curve directly.

### Q3: How is the solution validated independently?
The `src/validation.py` module is an independent audit engine that takes the output CSVs and checks the raw enrollments, rooms, and constraints from scratch without relying on any solver variables. It guarantees that direct student conflicts are strictly zero, room seat capacities are not exceeded, and all 13 institutional distribution constraints are satisfied.
